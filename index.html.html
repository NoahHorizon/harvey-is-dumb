The Scientific Case for Harvey Being Dumb

Harvey is a truly fascinating individual. Scientists have spent years studying the human brain, yet somehow Harvey continues to raise questions that science cannot answer. Every time you think he has reached his limit, he somehow discovers a brand-new way to make an obviously simple situation ten times more complicated.

One of Harvey's greatest talents is confidently being wrong. He can hear a perfectly reasonable explanation, ignore every single part of it, and then confidently choose the exact opposite answer. What makes this even more impressive is that Harvey rarely seems to notice that everyone around him is wondering what just happened.

Harvey also possesses an incredible ability to forget information almost immediately after learning it. You could explain something to him five times, write it down, make a presentation about it, and somehow he would still respond with, "Wait, what are we doing?"

Despite all of this, Harvey remains an important member of the group. Without him, there would be fewer ridiculous moments, fewer arguments over things that should be obvious, and significantly less entertainment.

So, is Harvey actually dumb? Probably not. But does he consistently provide enough evidence to make the case extremely difficult to defend? Absolutely.
